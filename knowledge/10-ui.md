# Pixel UI: panels, bars, icons and text

## Work at the game resolution

- Lay the UI out in logical pixels of the game camera (320x180, 640x360), then scale by
  whole numbers. 1.5x makes some pixels twice as wide as others and the whole interface
  looks broken.
- Judge every element at 1x over the busiest scene, never on an empty canvas.
- Grayscale test: screenshot the busiest fight, drop plain rectangles where the UI will
  go. This is where a red health bar gets lost against fire, a blue mana bar against
  water, an icon behind the player's hand.
- Text lives in the engine when the game needs to change it (translations, numbers).
  Art text is for titles, logos and fixed labels.

## One job per element, one strongest contrast

- The eye gets one strongest contrast. Reserve it for the selected action, not for the
  ornate border.
- Frames, trim and panels stay below the readable content in contrast. Trim that
  competes with the icon smears everything.
- Each element answers one question at a glance: how close am I to dying, which action
  is available, what is selected.

Starting sizes for a 320x180 game, before display scaling:

| piece | starting size | reserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ability icon | 16x16 | a readable silhouette and one dominant detail |
| button frame | 24x24 | a consistent border and space around the icon |
| bar fill | 80x8 | a separate frame so the fill can shrink alone |
| dialog panel | 160x72 | the longest label and a visible focus state |

At 3x a 24 px button covers 72 screen pixels. The clickable area is a separate
decision: make it forgiving, then test on the devices the game runs on.

## Panels: 9-slice

Four cut lines (left, top, right, bottom) split a small panel into nine pieces: four
corners that never scale, four edges that stretch on one axis, one centre that
stretches on both (or tiles). Same idea everywhere: Godot `NinePatchRect` and
`StyleBoxTexture` patch margins, Unity sprite border plus `Sliced` image type, CSS
`border-image-slice`.

- The margins are the border width, counted in source pixels. Count them again,
  including the dark outline, when a border looks 1 px off.
- Keep the border uniform on all four sides unless the design really needs otherwise.
  One number in the engine beats four.
- Keep the edge strips plain. Anything inside them (a rivet, a notch, a rope twist)
  gets stretched into a smear. Put details in the corners, or tile the edge instead of
  stretching it.
- Keep the centre flat or a pattern that tiles. A gradient in the centre stretches into
  a blur.
- A panel smaller than twice the margin overlaps its own corners. Enforce a minimum
  size of `2 x margin` per axis.
- Every state of the same button uses the same margins. If the hover sprite has a 2 px
  wider border, the content jumps when the pointer arrives.
- Draw at the size it is used, not bigger. A 24x24 or 32x32 source is plenty for a
  320x180 game.
- Integer scaling and nearest filtering, or the corners blur. That is a project
  setting, not an art problem.

Pixel bevels are cheap and read well: a 1 px lighter line on the lit side, a 1 px
darker line on the shadow side, then the body. Do not shade a panel in the middle; the
shape is flat and face-on.

## Bars

- Frame and fill are two images. The fill is cropped or masked, never scaled: scaling
  a 1 px highlight row turns it into a band.
- Fill recipe: base colour, one lighter row at the top, optional darker row at the
  bottom. Three values are enough. The 1 px dark outline belongs to the frame.
- Discrete resources get pips or segments. A segmented bar reads the value without a
  number and survives recolouring.
- A lagging "ghost" bar (the lost part stays for a few frames) makes damage readable.
- Never let colour alone carry meaning. Pair it with a number, an icon or a shape, and
  check the colours keep their distance in value, not only in hue.

## Buttons and states

| state | visible change |
| --- | --- |
| normal | stable border, icon readable without hovering |
| focus | outline or corner markers outside the border, so the box does not resize |
| pressed | content moves down 1 source pixel, border unchanged |
| disabled | lower contrast plus a lock or cooldown mark, not only a greyer colour |

Test all four states side by side at 1x, then trigger them in the real HUD. A focus
outline that disappears during a bright explosion is not a focus outline.

## Icons

- 16x16 or 8x8, always on the same grid. A UI icon is a tiny sprite: silhouette first,
  then one dominant detail. Heart, coin, key, potion must be recognisable from the
  solid shape alone.
- 1 px uniform stroke, one palette, no anti-aliasing.
- Icons sit on unknown backgrounds. Either give them a 1 px outline, or place them on
  a dark plate that belongs to the frame.
- Check every icon on the darkest and the brightest scene, not on grey.
- Noise and second thoughts do not survive 16 px. One idea per icon.

## Text

- Grids: 3x5 for tiny labels and digits (caps only, minimal punctuation), 5x7 for body
  text and numbers, 8x8 and up for titles.
- 1 px uniform stroke. Varying stroke weight per glyph kills legibility at this size.
- Disambiguate look-alikes: O vs 0, I vs 1 vs l, S vs 5, B vs 8. Give them
  deliberately different shapes.
- Keep counters open. A filled-in `a`, `e`, `o` reads as a black blob.
- One baseline for every glyph and digit. A 1 px slip is visible.
- 1 px spacing, then hand-kerning for the awkward pairs (VA, To, LT).
- Never anti-alias a bitmap font. Body text stays plain: no outline, no shadow, no
  gradient. Titles and logos are the exception and can take a 1 px outline, a 1 px
  drop shadow or a vertical gradient, one treatment at a time.
- Text needs content padding inside the panel, bigger than the patch margin. Test the
  longest label the game can produce, in every language it ships.

## Checklist before the UI ships

1. Does it read at 1x, and at the largest supported scale?
2. Over the busiest, brightest and darkest scene?
3. All button states side by side, all visible at once?
4. Longest label, smallest panel, largest panel, empty slot?
5. Integer scaling, nearest filtering, pixel snap on?
6. One border treatment and one palette across every screen?
7. Can a colour-blind player still read every bar and state?

## In p.xel

```xel
# requires: ramp steel, ramp blood, color ink
# panel: flat body, 1 px bevel, no middle shading
box 12 10 72 40 steel.1
hline 12 10 72 steel.3
vline 12 10 40 steel.3
hline 12 49 72 ink
vline 83 10 40 ink
rect 14 12 68 36 steel.0

# bar: frame and fill separate, fill cropped with clip, highlight row on top
rect 18 22 60 8 ink
box 19 23 58 6 blood.1
only color blood.1
  hline 19 23 58 blood.2
end
clip 47 23 30 6
  box 19 23 58 6 blood.0        # lost health, painted only inside the clip
end
```

- `clip` draws the lost part: paint the whole fill, then repaint the region right of
  the current value with the empty colour.
- `text 20 14 "HP" white font=5x7 shadow=ink` for the label. Body text stays plain.
- States are parts too: draw `button_a`, then `only color ...` + `replace` for pressed
  and disabled, or keep three parts with identical margins.
- Review with `--scale 4` and `--crop`, then look at the PNG, not the ascii: UI is
  judged on pixels, not on characters.
